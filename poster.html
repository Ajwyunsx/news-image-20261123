Climate diplomacy · Antalya

Climate pact collapses in Antalya as the gavel falls

COP31 closed on Sunday night with no pact at all. Nearly 200 countries could not agree to name the fossil fuels warming the planet — and left the money for adapting to it on the table.

The UN climate summit that was meant to end on Friday ended, at last, on Sunday evening — 49 hours late, and with nothing to sign. COP31 president Murat Kurum brought down the gavel on the Antalya Pact after the final plenary made plain that consensus did not exist.

Two questions broke the room. Whether the text should name coal, oil and gas as the causes of warming, which the European Union demanded and oil producers refused; and whether rich countries would commit to $120bn a year for adaptation, a figure that vanished from the final draft. Britain and Panama joined the objections.

The first collapse since Copenhagen

It is the first failure of a UN climate conference since Copenhagen in 2009, and the longest COP ever held. “It certainly fell short today,” said the UN's climate chief, Simon Stiell. “The world cannot afford a repeat.”

The Turkish presidency pointed to its action agenda and side deals as consolation. Few delegates in the hall treated them as such. With energy prices up sharply and the Iran war absorbing diplomatic attention, several delegations said the process itself now needs reform.

Talks move outside the tent

Frustrated by the inertia, a group of states said they would press ahead outside the COP process, with a coalition of the willing on electrification and a separate push on methane. Australia's climate minister, Chris Bowen, called the backdrop “the most complicated and fraught geopolitical environment in living memory”.

For the poorest delegations the arithmetic is blunter. The adaptation finance figure that disappeared from the text was the one line their capitals had come to Antalya to secure, and it will not be restored before the next conference, in 2027. Whether it hardens into a decade-long rout, as Copenhagen did, is now the question on every delegation’s mind.

United States

Democrats take 258 seats, their widest majority since 1992

A recount in North Carolina's fifth district gave Democrat Chuck Hubbard a 330-vote win over Republican Virginia Foxx, networks projected on Sunday, lifting his party to at least 258 House seats. Democrats gained 44 seats and hold the Senate.

Hakeem Jeffries is set to become the first Black speaker. Four races remain uncalled.

Party strategists describe the result as a backlash against the cost of living, not a mandate for any single programme. Democrats are preparing a 100-day agenda and a broad oversight schedule for the president's remaining two years.

“The largest majority any party in Congress has held this century.”Hakeem Jeffries, House Democratic leader

Space · Mercury

BepiColombo reaches Mercury, but its engine falters

Eight years and nine flybys after launch, the European–Japanese probe was captured by Mercury's gravity. Then ESA disclosed that a software problem had cut the braking burn short: “The thruster burn was shorter than planned,” it said.

The two orbiters separate on 9 December; science begins in April 2027. It is the first mission to put two craft in orbit around Mercury at once — ESA's planetary orbiter and Japan's Mio.

Controllers at ESOC in Darmstadt spent Monday reviewing telemetry. A slightly low orbit costs fuel the mission can ill spare.

“The thruster burn was shorter than planned.”European Space Agency, on the Mercury arrival
