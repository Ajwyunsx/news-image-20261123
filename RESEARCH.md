## 1. COP31 collapses in Antalya — LEAD

**What happened.** The UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, closed on the evening of
Sunday 22 November without an overarching agreement. COP31 President Murat Kurum struck
the gavel more than 49 hours after the conference was due to end — the longest COP on
record and the first collapse of a UN climate summit since Copenhagen in 2009. The draft
"Antalya Pact" lost a proposed $120bn-a-year adaptation finance target and avoided naming
coal, oil and gas; the EU refused to endorse a text that did not mention fossil fuels.
UN climate chief Simon Stiell said the outcome "certainly fell short".

**Why it matters.** Nearly 200 countries leaving with no deal weakens the COP process
itself, at a moment when energy prices and the Iran war have already scrambled climate
diplomacy. Adaptation money for vulnerable states is the concrete casualty.

## 3. Democrats clinch 258 House seats — largest majority since 1992

## 4. BepiColombo arrives at Mercury — with a thruster fault

**What happened.** The ESA–JAXA BepiColombo mission, a $2bn pair of orbiters launched in
2018, was captured by Mercury's gravity after nine planetary flybys. ESA confirmed the
orbit on Sunday, then disclosed that a software problem had cut the braking burn short —
"the thruster burn was shorter than planned" — with controllers at ESOC in Darmstadt
assessing telemetry. The two orbiters separate on 9–10 December; the science phase begins
in April 2027.

**Why it matters.** First mission to put two spacecraft around Mercury at once; it will
probe the planet's oversized iron core, its magnetic field and the ice hiding in polar
craters. A mis-sized orbit could cost fuel and time.
